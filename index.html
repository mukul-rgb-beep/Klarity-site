Why it’s different

Not all visualizations are created equal.

Most disk analyzers use sunburst charts. Research shows rectangular layouts are measurably better at comparing file sizes, so Klarity gives you both a treemap and an icicle view.

Treemap

Rectangles sized by file size. Your eye compares areas instantly. No angle math, no distortion at different depths.

Icicle

Horizontal bars aligned on the same baseline. Compare folders at the same level by looking at bar width. Hierarchy is depth, not angle.

Sunburst (others)

Encodes size as arc angle. Outer rings look visually larger even when they’re not. Brendan Gregg calls them “the new pie chart.”

What’s new

Klarity 1.3.1 — in App Review now.

  • A redesigned Klarity: a new light look (dark is one click away in Settings), a Finder-style sidebar with your locations, and an inspector that shows what’s inside the folder you pick.
  • Klarity now scans hidden folders such as your Library, so the space macOS calls “System Data” finally shows up. A new Library shortcut takes you straight there.
  • Photos libraries, video projects and virtual machines now count toward their folder’s size everywhere, not just when you open them.
  • New option in Settings › Appearance: colour folders by the age of most of their space, so big folders full of old files stand out even if one file changed today.
  • Sizes now show the space files actually take on your disk, so iCloud files that aren’t downloaded no longer count.
  • Klarity won’t move folders your Mac depends on, such as Library or Keychains, to the Trash, and it asks twice before trashing app data, iCloud folders or files inside photo and video libraries.
  • Clearer permission prompts: each one explains what Klarity measures.
