Age Suitability

Klarity Disk is a disk analyzer and system monitor for macOS. It shows you what is taking up space on your Mac and how much memory your apps are using. This page explains who the app is suitable for, and the one thing worth supervising on a shared Mac.

App Store rating

Klarity Disk is rated 4+ on the Mac App Store. That is Apple’s lowest age rating, and it carries no content advisories of any kind. The app contains nothing that is unsuitable for any age group.

It requires macOS 14.6 or later and costs $6.99, paid once.

What the app does not contain

There is nothing in Klarity Disk that needs a content warning, because there is no content in it at all beyond your own files. Specifically, the app has:

  • No user accounts and no sign-in
  • No social features, chat, or messaging
  • No user-generated content of any kind
  • No advertising
  • No in-app purchases and no subscriptions
  • No web content, and no links out to external sites from inside the app other than the support page

Klarity Disk also collects no data and makes no network connections at all. The app’s sandbox forbids both incoming and outgoing network access, so nothing leaves your Mac — there is no telemetry, no analytics, and nothing for anyone to see.

What the app can do

Klarity Disk can move files and folders to the macOS Trash. It only ever does this after you explicitly confirm, and for anything larger than 1 GB it asks you to slide to confirm as well. Klarity Disk never empties the Trash or deletes anything permanently itself. But the Trash is shared by everyone who uses the Mac, and anyone can empty it, so an item is only recoverable until that happens.

To scan a disk, the app asks once for access through Apple’s standard file picker, and remembers that grant so it does not have to ask again.

Guidance for families and shared Macs

Because Klarity Disk can move files to the Trash, on a shared or family Mac we recommend that a parent or the Mac’s administrator is the person using it, or is supervising while it is used. The app’s own action is reversible, but the outcome may not be: a younger user could move files someone else needs, and if the Trash is then emptied, by anyone, those files are gone for good.

macOS Screen Time applies to Klarity Disk exactly as it does to any other app. If you want to limit or block access to it, you can do so with Screen Time’s app limits and content restrictions in System Settings.
