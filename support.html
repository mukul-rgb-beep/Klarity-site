Frequently Asked Questions

Why can't Klarity see some folders?
Klarity is a Mac App Store app, so it can only read the places you have allowed. The first time you scan a disk, Klarity asks you to grant access to that disk. You do this once, and after that it can scan anywhere on it. Each external drive asks once too. You do not need to turn on Full Disk Access in System Settings. A few places that macOS keeps private from apps stay out of the map. The user guide shows the access prompt step by step.
Is my data sent anywhere?
No. Klarity runs 100% locally on your Mac. No data is collected, transmitted, or stored remotely. There are no analytics, no accounts, and no cloud services. See our Privacy Policy for details.
How do I drill down into a folder?
Double-click any folder in the treemap or icicle view to drill into it. Use the breadcrumb navigation at the top to go back up. You can also right-click any item for options like "Reveal in Finder" or "Drill Into".
Can I switch between visualization modes?
Yes. Go to Settings → Appearance → Disk Visualization and choose between Treemap (rectangles sized by file/folder size) or Icicle (stacked bars by hierarchy depth). Your preference is saved automatically.
Does Klarity delete files?
Not permanently. Klarity can move an item to the Trash, but only after you confirm, and a folder of 1 GB or more asks for a deliberate slide first. It never empties the Trash, and it refuses to trash the system folders your Mac depends on, so a mistake is recoverable until you empty the Trash yourself. You can also use "Reveal in Finder" to manage files by hand.
Can I monitor RAM usage?
Yes. Click Memory in the sidebar to see live memory use, and Klarity can show the figure in the menu bar too. Choose what the menu bar shows in Settings → Menu Bar → Display Mode, and how often the figures update in Settings → General → Refresh Interval. Settings → Appearance → RAM Monitor Display sets whether the Memory screen opens as a line chart or a gauge.
Does Klarity work with external drives and HDDs?
Yes. A drive that is plugged in appears under Devices in Klarity's sidebar. Click it to scan it. Klarity asks for access to each drive the first time. Scan speed depends on the drive: SSDs are quick, while traditional hard drives (HDDs) take longer because they read more slowly. A very large drive (4 TB+) can take a few minutes, and the map appears when the scan finishes. If a drive scans as empty, see the troubleshooting section of the user guide.
How do I see my Library folder and System Data?
Since version 1.3.1, Klarity scans hidden folders, so a Home scan includes ~/Library, the folder where most of what Storage settings calls System Data lives. To scan it on its own, click Library in Klarity's sidebar. See what System Data on a Mac actually is.
