Klarity Disk user guide

From your first scan to a tidier disk: how to read the map, what the colours mean, and how to clear space without breaking anything.

Written for Klarity Disk 1.3.1 on macOS 14.6 or later. Updated 21 November 2026.

Your first scan

Klarity lives in your Mac’s menu bar, at the top right of the screen. If its window is not already open, click the Klarity icon there and choose Open Klarity.

  1. Open the Disk Analyzer. Click Disk Analyzer in the sidebar.
  2. Choose what to scan. Click Scan Home to map your own files. For the whole disk, click Macintosh HD in the sidebar instead.
  3. Give access, once. The first time, Klarity asks to access your disk. Click Grant Access… and a macOS window opens, already pointing at the disk. Select the disk and click Grant Access.
  4. Wait for the map. The title counts items as it goes. A Home folder is usually quick. A whole disk takes longer, sometimes a few minutes, and you can press Cancel at any point.

Why macOS asks

Apps from the Mac App Store cannot read your files until you point them at a place yourself. The window where you choose the disk is drawn by macOS, not by Klarity, and Klarity only learns what you picked.

You are asked once per disk: once for your Mac’s own disk, and once for each external drive you scan. Klarity remembers after that. If you click Not Now, nothing is scanned and you can try again whenever you like. If you pick the wrong folder by mistake, Klarity says which one it needs and lets you choose again. The longer story is in why Klarity asks once per disk.

What Klarity can and cannot see

With your permission, Klarity can

  • Read the names, sizes and dates of the files and folders on that disk, so it can measure them.
  • Move an item to the Trash, but only when you ask and then confirm.

Klarity cannot

  • Send anything anywhere. It has no network access at all.
  • See a disk you have not given it.
  • Delete anything for good, or empty the Trash.
  • Look inside a few places macOS keeps private from apps. Those are simply missing from the map.

Reading the map

The map draws the folder you scanned as blocks. A block’s area is its share of the space, so the biggest blocks are where your space went.

Treemap and Icicle

There are two ways to draw the same scan, and the switch for them is in the toolbar.

Treemap packs everything into rectangles. A large folder becomes a frame with its name and size along the top and its own contents drawn inside, so you see two levels at once. It is the best view for spotting what is big.

Icicle stacks the same folders in rows. The top row is the folder you opened, and each row beneath holds the contents of the row above. A bar’s width is its size. It is the better view for comparing things that sit at the same level.

Getting around

Click a block
Selects it and shows its details in the inspector.
Double-click a folder
Opens it, so its contents fill the map.
Back, or Esc
Goes up one level.
The path under the title
Click any part of it to jump straight there.
⌘ Command Delete
Returns to the folder where the scan began.
Rescan, or ⌘ Command R
Measures the folder again.
Pinch on a trackpad
Zooms into a crowded corner. With a mouse, hold ⌘ Command and scroll. Once zoomed in, scroll to move around.
Right-click a block
Offers Reveal in Finder, Drill Into, Copy Path and Move to Trash.

The inspector

The panel on the right describes the block you clicked. With nothing selected, it describes the folder you are in.

Size
The space it takes on your disk, with its share of the folder around it and how many items it holds.
Last changed
When it was last modified, with a dot in its age colour.
Largest inside
The three biggest things in a folder. Click one to open or select it.
Show in Finder
Shows the item in Finder.
Move to Trash…
Starts the confirmation. Nothing moves until you confirm.

How deep a scan goes

To stay fast, Klarity opens two levels of folders on a first scan. Folders below that are still measured in full, so their sizes are right, but each is drawn as a single block. Double-click one and Klarity scans inside it.

To see more levels in one pass, raise Scan Depth in Settings › General. Deeper scans take longer.

What the colours mean

Klarity starts in Age colours. A block’s colour tells you how long it is since that file or folder last changed.

  1. This month Changed in the last 30 days
  2. This year 30 days to a year ago
  3. 1–2 yrs One to two years ago
  4. 2–3 yrs Two to three years ago
  5. 3+ yrs Untouched for three years or more

Large and red is the combination worth a look: a lot of space that nothing has touched in years. The same key sits under the map, next to a bar that shows how the whole folder divides by age. Point at a block and its colour lights up in the key.

Changed, not opened. The colour comes from the date something last changed, the Date Modified you see in Finder. A photo you look at often but never edit will still read as old. Old does not mean unneeded, so look before you remove anything.

How a folder gets its colour

A folder takes the colour of the newest thing inside it. That keeps folders you are working in looking fresh, but it also means one file saved today can turn a huge folder of old material blue.

If you would rather see where the old bulk is, turn on Colour folders by the age of most of their space in Settings › Appearance. A folder then takes the age of most of its contents by size. The trade-off: busy folders such as Documents can look older than they feel.

Folders below the scan depth have not been opened yet, so treat their colour as a rough guide until you double-click into them.

Type colours

Switch Age to Type in the toolbar and the map colours files by kind instead: images, video, audio, documents, code, archives, apps and so on. The key under the map changes to match. Folders get colours of their own, which are only there to tell neighbours apart.

The sidebar

The sidebar is where you choose what to look at. Click a location or a drive and Klarity switches to the Disk Analyzer and scans it.

Overview
Your Mac at a glance: memory, disk space, and the apps using the most memory.
Macintosh HD
Your whole startup disk. The figure and the thin bar show how full it is.
Home
Your own files: Documents, Pictures, Movies, Downloads and the rest.
Library
The folder macOS hides, where apps keep their data and caches. Most of what Storage settings calls System Data lives here.
Downloads, Applications
Two places that tend to collect forgotten things.
Devices
External drives that are plugged in. Each one asks for access the first time you scan it.

A scan of Macintosh HD covers your Mac’s own disk and leaves external drives out. Scan each drive from Devices.

For any other folder, click Choose Folder… in the toolbar and pick it. Settings sits at the bottom of the sidebar.

Finding big files

The map shows which folders are big. Large files in the toolbar shows which individual files are, as a plain list.

  • Sort with the menu at the top right: largest, smallest, name, oldest or newest first.
  • Filter by kind, such as Video, and by age: older than 30 days, 90 days, 6 months or a year.
  • Click a file to select it. Right-click it for Show in Finder or Move to Trash.
  • Close the list with the × to return to the map.

Open the folder first. Large Files only lists files in folders the scan has opened, and on a very big scan it is a shortlist that can miss some. For a thorough look, double-click into the folder you care about, then click Large files.

Clearing space safely

Klarity only ever moves things to the Trash. It never deletes anything outright and it never empties the Trash, so you can change your mind until you empty it yourself.

To remove something, right-click it and choose Move to Trash, or select it and use Move to Trash… in the inspector. Klarity asks you to confirm every time.

What Klarity will not let you do

Before it asks you anything, Klarity checks where the item lives.

It refuses
Folders your Mac or your account depends on: your Home folder itself, Library and its main folders, your keychains, iCloud Drive as a whole, the Applications folder and the macOS system folders.
It asks twice
App data and settings inside Library, files in iCloud Drive or another cloud folder (trashing them removes them from your other devices too), pieces from inside a Photos, Music or video library, and files that belong to another account. A warning explains the risk first, with Cancel as the default.
It asks once
Everything else, including everyday clean-up spots such as the contents of Library › Caches.

These checks catch the well-known dangers. They cannot know which of your own files matter to you. If you cannot say what something is, leave it where it is.

After you confirm

  • The map rescans so the block disappears. After a whole-disk scan, where a rescan takes a while, Klarity tells you to press ⌘ Command R when you are ready. If a block is ever still showing, ⌘ Command R clears it.
  • The space comes back only when you empty the Trash in Finder. Things trashed from an external drive stay on that drive until then.
  • To undo, open the Trash in Finder, select the item and choose File › Put Back.
  • A few things, such as apps installed from the App Store, are protected by macOS. Klarity says “Finder needs an admin password” and offers Reveal in Finder, so you can remove the item there with your password.

Menu bar and Memory

Klarity keeps a small icon in the menu bar, with or without figures beside it: R is the share of memory in use, D is how full your startup disk is.

Click the icon for a quick panel: memory in use, disk space used and free, and the apps using the most memory. Open Klarity brings up the main window. Quit closes Klarity completely, menu bar icon included.

The Memory screen

Memory in the sidebar shows the same picture in more detail, as a line chart or a gauge.

Active
Memory your apps are using right now.
Wired
Memory macOS itself needs and cannot give up.
Compressed
Memory macOS has squeezed down to make room.
Free
Memory nothing is using.
Swap Used
Disk space macOS is borrowing because memory ran short. A large figure means memory is tight.

Below that is a list of running apps with the memory each one uses. Type in the filter box to find one, and turn on System to include Apple’s own apps.

Klarity shows you memory; it does not quit apps or “clean” memory for you. The list covers apps rather than every background process, and its figures can differ a little from Activity Monitor, which groups memory differently.

Settings

Settings is at the bottom of the sidebar. These are the options worth knowing.

General

Launch at Login
Starts Klarity when you log in, so the menu bar icon is always there.
Refresh Interval
How often the memory figures update: every 1, 2, 5 or 10 seconds.
Scan Depth
How many folder levels a scan opens: 1 to 5, or Full. The default is 2. Full opens everything and is slow on a big disk.
Benchmark Mode
A testing tool for the developer. Leave it off.

Menu Bar

Show Usage Text
Shows or hides the figures beside the menu bar icon.
Display Mode
Which figures appear: RAM Only, Disk Only, RAM & Disk, or Icon Only.

Appearance

Mode
Light, Dark or Match System. Klarity starts in Light.
Theme Pack
Six accent colours: Klarity, Arctic, Ember, Verdant, Blush and Ocean. A pack changes buttons, highlights and icons. The map’s colours stay the same in every pack.
Disk Visualization
Whether the map opens as a Treemap or an Icicle, and the folder-colour option described under colours.
RAM Monitor Display
Whether the Memory screen opens on the line chart or the gauge.

Troubleshooting

An external drive scans as empty

In version 1.3.1, if Klarity was already open when you plugged in a drive you have scanned before, the map can come up empty. Quit Klarity from its menu bar panel, then open it again with the drive connected.

If Klarity asks for access to the drive, select the drive itself in the macOS window, not a folder somewhere else. More on drives in what to do when an external drive is full.

I cannot find System Data

System Data is a label in macOS Storage settings, not a folder, so there is no block with that name. Most of it lives in your Library folder: click Library in the sidebar and look at the biggest blocks, usually Caches, Developer, Application Support and Containers.

Some of what Storage settings counts is not made of files an app can list, so Klarity’s total can come out lower. What System Data on a Mac actually is explains what is in there and what is safe to clear.

Klarity’s sizes differ from Finder’s

Klarity shows the space a file really takes on your disk. Finder’s Get Info window shows two figures, and Klarity matches the one marked “on disk”. Two cases where the difference is large:

  • iCloud files that are not downloaded to this Mac take almost no space, so Klarity counts them as almost nothing.
  • Virtual machine and disk image files often claim more than they use. Klarity shows what they use.

Free space can differ too. Finder counts space that macOS can clear on demand as available, while Klarity shows what is free right now. See purgeable space, explained.

A folder shows as one block

It is below the scan depth: measured, but not opened. Double-click it to scan inside, or raise Scan Depth in Settings › General.

A big old folder looks new

One recently changed file inside it is enough to colour the whole folder as new. Turn on Colour folders by the age of most of their space in Settings › Appearance, or double-click into the folder to see each item’s own colour.

Klarity will not move something to the Trash

If the message says the item is part of macOS or your account, Klarity is protecting it on purpose. If it mentions an admin password, use Reveal in Finder and delete it there. On a drive formatted for Windows (NTFS), a Mac can read files but not change them, so nothing on it can be trashed by Klarity or by Finder.

I trashed files but the free space did not change

Files in the Trash still take up space. Empty the Trash in Finder and the space comes back.

The disk figure in the menu bar looks out of date

Quit Klarity from its menu bar panel and open it again. The map is not affected: it always measures at the moment you scan.

I cannot find Klarity’s window

Click the Klarity icon in the menu bar and choose Open Klarity. Klarity runs from the menu bar, so it may not show in the Dock.
