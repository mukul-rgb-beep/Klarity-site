Old iPhone Backups Filling Your Mac? Find and Clear Them

Every iPhone and iPad you have backed up to this Mac may still have a full copy sitting in a hidden folder, including phones you sold years ago. How to see them, what to keep, and how to delete the rest the right way.

The biggest folder you have never seen

If you have ever plugged an iPhone or iPad into your Mac and backed it up, there is a copy of that device on your disk. Not a few settings: a backup holds most of the data and settings that were on the device when it was made. A well-used phone can leave a backup of tens of gigabytes.

Finder keeps one backup per device, and replaces it with a fresh one each time that device backs up again. That part is tidy. The trouble is what happens when you stop using a device. You trade the phone in, the new one restores from iCloud, and the old phone’s backup simply stays on the Mac. Nothing ever replaces it, and nothing ever asks you about it. An iPad you gave to a relative, a phone from two upgrades ago, a work phone from a previous job: each can still be there, at full size.

You will not stumble on them. They live inside your Library folder, which Finder hides, and Storage settings shows a coloured bar of broad categories, where a forgotten backup is easy to miss. So the space goes missing and you cannot see where. That is the real problem here: not that backups are big, but that they are out of sight.

Where Finder keeps iPhone and iPad backups

Apple’s support page gives the location. In your home folder, inside the hidden Library folder:

Library → Application Support → MobileSync → Backup

To open it in Finder, choose Go → Go to Folder and type ~/Library/Application Support/MobileSync/Backup/. Inside, each device has its own folder. Do not expect to recognise them: the folders are named with long codes of letters and numbers, not “Anna’s iPhone”. And Finder will not tell you how much space each one takes until you select a folder and wait for Get Info to add it up, one at a time.

So Apple tells you where they are. It does not show you which ones are big, or which ones are years old. That is the gap a picture of your disk fills.

See every backup at a glance with Klarity

Klarity, the Mac disk space analyzer I build, draws a folder as a treemap: every folder inside it is a rectangle, and its area is the space it takes. Since version 1.3.1 it scans hidden folders too, Library included, so phone backups are in the picture instead of left out of it.

  1. Click Library in Klarity’s sidebar.
  2. Look for Application Support. If you have phone backups on this Mac, a large rectangle called MobileSync usually takes up most of it.
  3. Double-click MobileSync, then Backup. Each rectangle is now one device’s backup, sized by the space it uses.

The colour tells you the rest. By default Klarity colours each rectangle by how long since it last changed: blue for this month, through green, gold and orange, to red for three years or more. A backup changes every time its device backs up, so the phone you use now is usually blue or green, and a phone you stopped using shows the age of its last backup. A big gold, orange or red rectangle in that folder is almost always a device you no longer carry.

Click any rectangle and the inspector on the right shows its size, its share of the folder, how many items it holds and exactly when it last changed. That is usually enough to match each code-named folder to a device: the one that last changed three years ago is probably the phone you traded in back then.

Two honest notes. Klarity measures the space a backup really takes on disk, which is the same figure Finder shows as “on disk”. And Klarity does not open backups or read what is inside them; it cannot tell you which phone a folder came from, only how big and how old it is. Finder fills in the names, as below.

Before you delete anything

A deleted backup is gone for good; Apple says plainly that you cannot recover it. So, before you remove one, check three things:

  • Keep the backup of the phone you use now, unless you are sure it is backed up elsewhere. On the iPhone, open Settings, tap your name, then iCloud → iCloud Backup, and look for a recent date. If iCloud Backup is off, the copy on your Mac may be the only one.
  • Encrypted backups hold more. If you ticked “Encrypt local backup”, Apple notes the backup can include your saved passwords, Wi-Fi settings, website history, Health data and call history. Years of Health data in particular may exist nowhere else. Never delete the only copy.
  • Old phones can still matter. If an old phone held photos or messages that never made it to iCloud or the new phone, its backup is the last place they exist. If in doubt, keep that one and clear the rest.

Delete old backups the right way: Finder

Finder is the right tool for the delete, because it shows what Klarity cannot: the device name and the date of each backup. Following Apple’s steps:

  1. Connect an iPhone or iPad to the Mac with a cable. It can be your current one; the list shows every backup on this Mac, not just that device’s.
  2. Select the device in the Finder sidebar.
  3. On the General tab, click Manage Backups.
  4. Right-click a backup you no longer need and choose Delete, then confirm.

The space comes back straight away: a backup deleted this way does not go to the Trash first. If you prefer not to plug anything in, recent versions of macOS also list backups in Storage settings: open System Settings → General → Storage and click the info button next to iOS Files, where you can select an old backup and delete it.

Then rescan Library in Klarity. The rectangle should be gone, and the free space shown at the bottom of the window should have grown by about the same amount.

Why not just delete it from Klarity?

You can. Right-click the backup’s rectangle and choose Move to Trash. But Klarity treats your Library folder with care, because apps keep their data there. Before it moves anything from Application Support, it shows an extra warning that you have to get past, with Cancel as the default. A backup is also usually over 1 GB, and for folders that large Klarity asks you to slide to confirm. Only then does the backup go to the Trash, and the space comes back when you empty it.

That is two deliberate steps on purpose, and it is still the long way round. Klarity cannot tell you whose backup you are holding; Finder can. So my advice is simple: use Klarity to find the backups and see what they cost you, and Finder’s Manage Backups to delete them.

Questions people ask

Where are iPhone backups stored on a Mac?

In a hidden folder in your Library: Library, then Application Support, then MobileSync, then Backup. Each phone or iPad gets its own folder, named with a long code rather than the device name. To open it, choose Go to Folder in the Finder Go menu and type ~/Library/Application Support/MobileSync/Backup/.

Is it safe to delete old iPhone backups from a Mac?

Yes, for devices you no longer own or have already set up again, as long as you keep at least one recent backup of the phone you use now, or iCloud Backup is on. A deleted backup cannot be recovered, and an encrypted one can hold your saved passwords and Health data, so never delete the only copy.

What is the best way to delete an iPhone backup on a Mac?

Use Finder. Connect an iPhone or iPad, select it in the Finder sidebar, click Manage Backups on the General tab, then right-click the old backup and choose Delete. The list shows the device name and the date of each backup, which the folder names do not.

Can Klarity delete iPhone backups?

Klarity shows how big each backup is and how long since it last changed, and it can move one to the Trash. Because backups sit inside your Library, it asks you to confirm an extra warning first. For deleting, Finder is the better tool, because it shows which device each backup belongs to.
