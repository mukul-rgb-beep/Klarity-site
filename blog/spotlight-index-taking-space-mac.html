Spotlight Index Taking Up Space on a Mac? What to Do

Free space dropped after the update? Part of it can be Spotlight building its index again. What Apple says about it, how long to wait, and what you can safely do.

Why the space went after the update

After a macOS update, Spotlight indexes your Mac again, and on at least one Mac that work took a lot of room while it ran. Apple’s page on Spotlight indexing says indexing happens “when you first set up your device, after you install a software update or upgrade, and as data changes on your device.” It also says: “Depending on the amount of data on your device, Spotlight indexing can take hours or even days.”

The index is what lets Spotlight find “files, folders, and apps”, along with “documents, email, text messages, contacts, photos” and more. It is “a local, private index that stays on your device and isn’t shared with Apple or your other devices.” Apple does not say which Storage category the index counts in; System Data, Apple’s catch-all for files that fit no other category, is the likely place. What System Data is explains that grey bar.

How big it gets

Apple gives no size for the index, so all I can give you is measurements:

  • On my own Mac, running the macOS 27.3 beta, the part of the index kept in my home folder (Library → Metadata → CoreSpotlight) is 5.2 GB. macOS may keep more of it outside the home folder, which I did not measure.
  • One owner on the MacRumors forums watched Spotlight reindex on macOS 27 and measured about 40 GB in use next to an 8 GB index while it ran, and 8.7 GB in that same Library folder once it had finished. That is one Mac and one person’s figures, not Apple’s.

On these two Macs, a few gigabytes stayed, and much more came and went while indexing ran. Apple’s notes for macOS Tahoe 26.6 said that update “optimizes the Spotlight index in preparation for macOS 27”, and Apple lists “Better search in Spotlight and Mail” among the macOS 27 changes, which suggests this year’s upgrade gave Spotlight more to do than usual.

First, wait. Then look at what is really yours

If free space dropped in the day or two after an update, give Spotlight time to finish, as Apple says, “hours or even days”. Restart once. Then check Storage settings again. Apple adds that “Incremental updates after that are much faster.”

If the space has not come back after that, the index is probably not your problem, and the space is likely in your own files. Keep big folders out of the index

If you have large folders you never search, such as an archive drive, virtual machines or old project folders, you can tell Spotlight to skip them. Apple calls this the list of “locations that Spotlight is prevented from searching”:

  1. Choose Apple menu → System Settings, then click Spotlight (or Siri & Spotlight) in the sidebar.
  2. Scroll down and click Search Privacy (or Spotlight Privacy) at the bottom of the window.
  3. Drag the folder or disk into the list, or click the add button (+) and choose it.
  4. Click Done.

The trade-off: Spotlight will no longer find anything in that folder. Apple does not say how much space this saves, so do it for folders you never search, not as a clean-up trick.

If search is broken: index a folder again

Apple’s page also describes “rebuilding part of the Spotlight index” when search keeps missing files in one disk or folder. It uses the same list:

  1. Open Search Privacy as above and add the disk (such as Macintosh HD) or folder to the list.
  2. “Wait a few seconds.” Then select it in the list and click the remove button (–).
  3. Click Done and quit System Settings. Apple: “Spotlight now reindexes the contents of the disk or folder.”

Only do this to fix search, not to save space. Reindexing a whole disk can take hours, and it may need extra room while it runs, as after an update.

What not to do

  • Do not delete index folders by hand. The index belongs to macOS, including the part in your Library folder. Apple’s way to rebuild is the one above.
  • Do not clear things while the space is still moving. Caches and the index both rebuild after an update; why free space drops after an update covers what comes back by itself.

Questions people ask

Does the Spotlight index take up space on a Mac?

Yes. Apple gives no size, but the index is stored on your Mac, and one forum owner measured it using much more room while it reindexed. On the author’s Mac the part in the home folder is 5.2 GB; one forum owner measured 8.7 GB in the same folder once indexing had finished.

How long does Spotlight indexing take after a macOS update?

Apple says: “Depending on the amount of data on your device, Spotlight indexing can take hours or even days.” Later updates to the index are much faster.

Can I delete the Spotlight index to free space?

Do not delete it by hand. To make Spotlight index a disk or folder again, add it to Search Privacy in Spotlight settings, wait a few seconds, then remove it. That fixes search; it does not save space while the new index is built.

How do I stop Spotlight indexing a folder?

In System Settings, open Spotlight (or Siri & Spotlight), click Search Privacy (or Spotlight Privacy) and add the folder or disk. Spotlight will no longer search it.
