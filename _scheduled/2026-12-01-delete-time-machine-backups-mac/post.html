How to Delete Time Machine Backups on a Mac, Safely

A backup dragged to the Trash can sit there for hours. What Time Machine clears by itself, how to remove the rest properly, and how to tell whether backups are your space problem at all.

Two different things get called a Time Machine backup

When people say Time Machine is taking up space, they mean one of two things, and the fix is different for each.

  • Backups on your backup disk. The external drive or network drive you chose for Time Machine. These fill that drive, not your Mac.
  • Local snapshots on your Mac itself. Apple says Time Machine “also stores some of its backups on your Mac”. These are the ones that seem to eat your Mac’s own storage.

So first decide which disk is full. If it is the backup drive, most of this page says the same thing: leave it to Time Machine. If it is your Mac, look at what else is on it before you delete anything; the next section shows how.

First, check that backups are the problem

Snapshots sound guilty. They are hidden, they are large, and they belong to something called Time Machine. But Apple is plain about how they are counted: “Your Mac counts the space used by snapshots as available storage.” Apple says macOS removes them when the room is needed. That does not always happen promptly, so a sudden drop in free space can be snapshots. A disk that has filled slowly over months is more often full of ordinary things: old video, a phone backup, a virtual machine you forgot about.

Backups on the backup disk: let Time Machine do it

Apple’s answer here is short. As your backup disk fills up, Time Machine deletes the oldest backups to make room. It keeps hourly backups for the past 24 hours, daily backups for the past month, and weekly backups for as long as there is space.

That means a full backup disk is normal. It is how Time Machine is meant to run, and you do not need to make room for it.

If the disk is too small to hold even your recent backups, Apple suggests two things: leave out what does not need backing up (System Settings → General → Time Machine → Options), or connect a new backup disk. If you are choosing one, this guide to Time Machine drives covers what works on macOS 27.

Why the Trash is the wrong tool

This is the search that brings most people here. Someone drags an old backup to the Trash to make room, and then the Trash will not empty, or sits for hours and seems to do nothing.

A backup is not an ordinary folder. Each one is a full picture of your Mac at one moment. On older backup disks macOS protected them, and the Trash could sit for a day or refuse to empty. On today’s disks it is still the slow way: Howard Oakley, who documents Time Machine at The Eclectic Light Company, writes that trashing a backup can take an age and brings no benefit.

If a backup is in your Trash right now: open the Trash, Control-click the backup and choose Put Back, which returns it to the backup disk. This advice comes from the older kind of backup disk, and I have not tested it on macOS 27. If the Trash had already started emptying, treat that backup as incomplete.

If you really want one backup gone: Apple no longer publishes steps for today’s backup disks; its page on the subject stops at macOS 10.15. Oakley describes the method that remains: open the backup disk in the Finder, Control-click the dated backup, and choose the command that deletes it immediately. It skips the Trash and cannot be undone. I have not tested it on macOS 27, so read his notes first. You cannot remove single files from inside a backup; backups are read-only.

If you want to start again from nothing: erase the backup volume in Disk Utility and let Time Machine make a fresh first backup. Check that you have selected the right one: if the disk also holds other files, erasing the whole disk deletes those too. You lose every older version of every file, and you have no backup at all until the new one finishes. Do it only when your Mac is healthy.

Snapshots on your Mac: mostly, do nothing

Apple says Time Machine saves a snapshot of your disk about every hour and keeps it for 24 hours. It keeps one more, of your last successful backup, until the space is needed, and it saves another before any macOS update. Then it deletes them by itself, as they age or as space is needed for other things.

So the usual advice is to wait a day. If free space dropped after an update or a big clear-out and has not come back, that is often snapshots holding on to the old files; purgeable space on a Mac explains how that space is counted.

If you want to see them, or remove them yourself, there are two ways that need no Terminal.

  1. Disk Utility. Open Disk Utility, choose View → Show APFS Snapshots, and select your Mac’s data volume in the sidebar (usually Macintosh HD, or Macintosh HD - Data). The snapshots are listed at the bottom of the window. First check that your backup disk has a recent backup, because a deleted snapshot cannot be brought back. Then select the ones you do not want and click the Delete Snapshot button. Leave the most recent one: Time Machine may rely on it for the next backup.
  2. Time Machine settings. Apple’s own method: in System Settings → General → Time Machine, click Options and set Backup Frequency to Manually. Wait a few minutes for the snapshots to be deleted, then set it back to automatic.

Do set it back. Switching Time Machine off for good to save a few gigabytes trades a small, temporary problem for the day you need a file and have no backup.

A short routine

  1. Decide which disk is full: the backup drive, or your Mac.
  2. Backup drive full: do nothing. Time Machine removes the oldest backups itself.
  3. A backup sitting in the Trash: Put Back, then leave it alone.
  4. Mac full: scan it and deal with the biggest, oldest things first. The hourly snapshots clear by themselves within a day.
  5. Only if you still need room this minute: delete older snapshots in Disk Utility, and keep the newest.

Questions people ask

Can I delete Time Machine backups by dragging them to the Trash?

It is better not to. The Trash can take a very long time over a backup, and on older backup disks it could refuse to empty. If a backup is already in the Trash, open the Trash, Control-click the backup and choose Put Back, which returns it to the backup disk. Then leave old backups to Time Machine, which removes the oldest ones when the disk fills up.

Does Time Machine delete old backups automatically?

Yes. Apple says that as your backup disk fills up, Time Machine deletes the oldest backups to make room. A full backup disk is normal and needs no action from you.

Do Time Machine local snapshots use up my Mac’s storage?

They take space, but Apple says your Mac counts that space as available, and Time Machine deletes snapshots as they age or when the space is needed for something else. Each hourly snapshot is kept for about 24 hours.

How do I delete local snapshots without Terminal?

Open Disk Utility, choose View, then Show APFS Snapshots, select your Mac’s data volume, and delete the snapshots you do not need from the list at the bottom, keeping the most recent. Or open System Settings, General, Time Machine, click Options, set Backup Frequency to Manually, wait a few minutes, then set it back to automatic.
