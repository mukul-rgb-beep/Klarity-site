Mac Says “Your Disk Is Almost Full”? What to Do First

The message tells you the disk is nearly full and not much else. Here is what to do in the first ten minutes, what to leave alone, and how to find what actually took the space.

What the message means

A notification slides in: Your disk is almost full. Under it, a line about saving space by optimizing storage, and a Manage button.

It means what it says. The disk your Mac starts from is close to its limit. Apple does not publish the exact amount of free space that triggers it, and you do not need the figure: when you see the message, the Mac is already short.

It is worth acting on today. Apple’s support pages name three effects of a full disk: a Mac that runs slowly, files that will not download or copy, and macOS updates that fail to install.

You do not need to delete anything in a hurry. The first steps are low-risk, and take about ten minutes.

The first ten minutes

These come from Apple’s own advice, in the order I would do them.

  1. Empty the Trash. Files in the Trash still take their full space. Apple puts it plainly: the space “doesn’t become available until you empty the Trash”. Look through it first, then choose Finder → Empty Trash.
  2. Open Downloads. In the Finder choose Go → Downloads and sort by size. Installers you have already run and files you downloaded twice are the usual finds.
  3. Read the recommendations. Open System Settings → General → Storage (the Manage button on the message should take you there). Apple offers three: Store in iCloud, Optimize Storage and Empty Trash automatically. The third suits most people: it erases items that have sat in the Trash for more than 30 days. The first moves files to iCloud, which needs iCloud storage you may have to pay for, so read it before you switch it on.

That can be enough to make the message go away. If the disk fills again within weeks, though, something large is still there, and the message never says what.

Find what actually filled the disk

Storage settings shows categories: Documents, Photos, System Data. A category is not a folder you can open. (Documents has a File Browser, but it covers only part of the disk.) To clear real space you need to know which folder, and which files.

What not to do

  • Do not delete folders in Library that you do not recognise. The odd names often belong to apps you use every day. Group Containers is the usual example: it looks like junk and can hold your Outlook mail and your notes.
  • Do not switch off Time Machine to save space. Its snapshots clear by themselves, and a backup matters most on a Mac that is under strain. See how to delete Time Machine backups safely.
  • Do not paste commands from a forum into Terminal. A command that deletes cannot be undone, and it does not ask.
  • Do not ignore it. A full disk is easier to fix today than on the day an update will not install.

If the space does not come back

You emptied the Trash, and the number barely moved. Two things can cause that.

  • Snapshots. With Time Machine on, your Mac keeps hourly snapshots for about 24 hours, and a file you deleted can stay in one until it ages out. Wait a day and look again; this guide shows how to clear snapshots sooner.
  • System caches. Apple suggests starting the Mac in safe mode, which clears certain system caches, then restarting normally. The gain is temporary: the caches come back as they are needed.

How much free space is enough

Apple gives no figure. The largest single demand most Macs meet is a macOS upgrade, and by my estimate macOS 27 needed about 40 GB free to install. If you can keep that much clear, upgrades should have room and you are unlikely to see this message. On a small disk that is a real share of the whole, which is why living with a 256 GB Mac takes a routine, not one clear-out.

Questions people ask

What should I do first when my Mac says the disk is almost full?

Look through the Trash and empty it, remove installers and duplicates from Downloads, then open System Settings, General, Storage and read Apple’s recommendations. These are low-risk if you check what you delete; emptying the Trash cannot be undone. After that, find what filled the disk before you delete anything else.

Why is my Mac disk still full after emptying the Trash?

With Time Machine on, your Mac keeps hourly snapshots for about 24 hours, and a deleted file can stay in one until it ages out. Wait a day and look again. If the space still has not returned, something large is still on the disk, and a scan will show where.

How much free space should a Mac have?

Apple gives no figure. A macOS upgrade is the largest single demand: by this site’s estimate, macOS 27 needed about 40 GB free to install. Keeping that much clear is a sensible margin.

Is it safe to delete files from the Library folder?

Not unless you know what they are. Library holds the data of apps you use every day, including mail, notes and settings. Folders left behind by apps you have removed are the safe exception.
