Rosetta Ends After macOS 27: Clear Out Your Intel Apps

Apple has confirmed that macOS 27 is the last version to run Intel-only apps on Apple silicon Macs. How to find yours, decide what to keep, and clear the rest out properly.

macOS 27 is the last stop for Intel apps

Apple moved the Mac to its own chips years ago, and Rosetta is what has kept older Intel apps running since. That ends soon. Apple told developers on 1 September that macOS 27 is the final release to support Rosetta: “Intel-only apps will no longer run on Mac computers with Apple silicon after this update.” The one exception Apple names is older, unmaintained games that rely on Intel-based frameworks.

You may have had a warning already. Since macOS 26.4, opening an app that relies on Rosetta can bring up a notice that it will not open in a future release of macOS. And when you upgrade to macOS 27, the upgrade removes Rosetta, Six Colors reports; the first time you open an Intel app afterwards, macOS asks to install it again. Not every app asks. In 9to5Mac’s testing, some menu bar utilities simply stopped working, with no message at all.

So your Intel apps still work on macOS 27, but this is the year to sort them out: update the ones you use, replace the ones that will never be updated, and clear out the ones you forgot you had.

Find out which of your apps are Intel

macOS keeps the list for you. According to Six Colors:

  1. Open System Settings → General → About.
  2. Click Details next to Intel-based Apps.
  3. You get the apps that need Rosetta, and when you last opened each one in the past year.

To check one app, select it in Finder and choose File → Get Info; an Intel app shows a warning next to Kind. The list covers apps, not every small background part an app installs, which is why a printer driver or a menu bar helper can still surprise you.

Decide app by app

  • Update it. Check the App Store or the developer’s site for a newer version. Many apps were rebuilt for Apple silicon long ago, and the old copy on your Mac simply predates it.
  • Replace it. If the developer has stopped updating it, look for an alternative now, while the old app still opens and you can move your work across.
  • Remove it. If the list in System Settings shows you have not opened it this year, you probably will not miss it.
  • Keep it, for now. An app you still need keeps working on macOS 27. Just know that it will not open after you move to the next major release.
  • Games. Apple says older, unmaintained games that rely on Intel-based frameworks will keep working.

Clear them out properly

Move the app to the Trash, from Finder or with a right-click and Move to Trash in Klarity. Questions people ask

Will Intel apps stop working in macOS 27?

No. macOS 27 still runs them through Rosetta, although the upgrade removes Rosetta and macOS asks to install it again the first time you open an Intel app. Apple says macOS 27 is the final release to support Rosetta, so Intel-only apps will not open on Apple silicon Macs after it, apart from some older games.

How do I see which apps on my Mac are Intel-based?

Open System Settings, then General, then About, and click Details next to Intel-based Apps. The list shows the apps that need Rosetta and when you last opened each one. For a single app, select it in Finder and choose File, then Get Info.

Can Klarity tell which apps are Intel-based?

No. Klarity shows how big each app is, how long since it last changed, and what it left behind in your Library folder. The Intel-based Apps list in System Settings is the place to check which apps need Rosetta.

Is it safe to delete Intel apps I no longer use?

Yes. Removing an app does not remove the documents you made with it. Move the app to the Trash, then look in your Library folder for the support files it left. Keep any app you still rely on until you have a replacement that runs natively.
