Group Containers on a Mac: What Is Safe to Delete?

A hidden folder with unreadable names and, often, tens of gigabytes inside. What Group Containers is, which parts belong to apps you no longer have, and what to leave alone.

What the Group Containers folder is

Inside your Library folder, which macOS hides, there is a folder called Group Containers. Open it and you find dozens of folders with names like UBF8T346G9.Office and group.com.apple.notes. It can be one of the largest things in your Library, and nothing about it says what is safe to touch.

Apple’s developer documentation describes it simply: it is where apps from the same developer keep the files they share with each other. Word and Outlook share one.

The names follow two patterns. Some start with a short code that identifies the developer; UBF8T346G9 is Microsoft’s. Others start with the word group followed by the developer’s web address written backwards, so group.com.apple.notes belongs to Apple’s Notes (com.apple is apple.com turned round).

The folder next to it, Containers, is the same idea for one app at a time: each app’s private data.

Why it gets so big

Because it is not junk. It is the real thing.

  • Outlook. Microsoft says Outlook for Mac keeps a copy of your mail in a profile inside the UBF8T346G9.Office folder. Years of mail with attachments can run to tens of gigabytes.
  • Notes. The Notes database, with the notes kept on this Mac and the ones synced from iCloud, lives in group.com.apple.notes.
  • Chat apps. Telegram keeps your sign-in here, and chat apps can keep downloaded photos and videos here too.

There is a second reason, and it is the useful one. Apple’s documentation says macOS creates one of these folders the first time an app needs it and “never removes it”. Delete the app, and its folder stays. After a few years a Mac collects the leftovers of many apps it has run.

See which ones are big, and which are old

The Finder shows this folder as a list of odd names, with no sizes unless you turn them on. Klarity, the Mac disk space analyzer I build, draws the whole thing as a map. Every folder is a rectangle, and its size on screen is the space it takes on disk. Since version 1.3.1 it scans the folders macOS hides, and Library has its own shortcut in the sidebar.

Click Library, then open Group Containers. Two things show at once:

  • Size. One or two rectangles usually fill most of the picture. Those are the only ones worth your time.
  • Age. Each rectangle is coloured by how long since anything in it changed: blue for this month, through green, gold and orange, to red for three years or more. An app you use every day is blue. A red folder has not been touched for years, and often belongs to an app you no longer have. Check before you delete.

One honest caveat. macOS guards the data apps keep here, so that one app cannot quietly read another’s. When Klarity looks into another app’s folder, macOS may ask you whether to allow it. If you say no, or if the folder belongs to one of the apps macOS protects most closely, that folder can show as much smaller than it is. What is safe to delete

Usually safe: the folder of an app you have removed, when no other app from the same developer is still installed and you do not want that app’s data back. One folder can serve several apps: remove Word and keep Outlook, and the Office folder is still in use. Move the folder to the Trash and leave it there for a week before you empty the Trash, in case something turns out to need it.

Not safe: anything that starts with group.com.apple. Those belong to macOS and Apple’s own apps, including ones with no icon in your Applications folder.

Not safe: anything that belongs to an app you still use.

  • Outlook and Office. Microsoft’s own uninstall instructions warn that Outlook data is removed when you move these folders, and tell you to back them up first. To make Outlook smaller, remove an account or old mail from inside Outlook. Deleting mail in Outlook usually deletes it from your mail account as well, so only remove what you no longer want anywhere.
  • Notes. Leave group.com.apple.notes alone. Your notes are in it.
  • Chat apps. You will probably be signed out, and may lose whatever the app kept only on this Mac. Many chat apps have a storage setting that clears old photos and videos. Use that.

Not sure whose it is? Search the web for the folder’s name before you decide. If you still cannot tell, leave it. A folder you do not understand is rarely the one that is filling your disk.

And do not delete the Group Containers folder itself. Apps you use every day would lose their data all at once.

How Klarity handles the risky part

You can act from the same window: select a rectangle and choose Move to Trash. Because these folders hold app data, Klarity treats them with more care than a folder of old downloads. macOS guards them too, and may not let Klarity move one; if that happens, click Show in Finder and move it to the Trash there.

  • It refuses to trash the Group Containers folder itself, or the Library folder.
  • For a folder inside Group Containers it shows a warning first and then asks you to confirm, so you get two chances to back out.
  • A folder of 1 GB or more needs a deliberate slide to confirm, not a click.
  • It only ever moves things to the Trash. It never empties the Trash for you, so you can put a folder back.

A quick routine

  1. Scan your Library folder and open Group Containers.
  2. Ignore everything small. Look at the one or two big rectangles.
  3. Big and red, from an app you removed, with no other app from that developer installed: move it to the Trash, and empty the Trash a week later.
  4. Big and blue, from an app you use: shrink it from inside that app, or leave it.
  5. Never delete the Group Containers folder itself, or anything that starts with group.com.apple.

Questions people ask

Is it safe to delete Group Containers on a Mac?

Not the whole folder, and not the folders of apps you still use: they hold real data such as Outlook mail, your Notes and chat app data. It is usually safe to move a folder to the Trash when it belongs to an app you have removed and no other app from that developer is still installed. macOS never removes those leftovers by itself.

What is the difference between Containers and Group Containers?

Both are in your Library folder. Containers holds each app’s private data, one folder per app. Group Containers holds data that several apps from the same developer share, such as the Office apps.

Why is Group Containers so large?

Usually because of one or two apps. Outlook keeps a copy of your mail there, and chat apps can keep photos and videos there. Folders left behind by apps you have removed add to it, because macOS does not delete them.

Will deleting a group container free space right away?

Only after you empty the Trash. It is worth waiting a week before you do, so you can put the folder back if an app turns out to need it.
