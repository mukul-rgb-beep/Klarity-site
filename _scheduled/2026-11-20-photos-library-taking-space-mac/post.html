Why Your Photos Library Takes So Much Space on a Mac

Photos keeps your whole library behind one icon in Pictures. What makes it big, what Optimize Mac Storage really does, and how to find and deal with the old libraries you forgot.

The biggest thing on many Macs is one icon in Pictures

Open your Pictures folder in Finder and the Photos library looks like a single file: one icon, Photos Library. Behind that icon can sit a hundred gigabytes or more, and on a lot of Macs it is the largest single thing on the disk. Storage settings gives Photos one coloured stripe in its bar, but not what is making it big, or whether an older library is sitting next to it.

That is the real problem. Not that photos take space — of course they do — but that you cannot see which part of it is your living library, which part is a library you forgot years ago, and which part is the same pictures stored twice. Here is why the library grows, what Optimize Mac Storage really does, and how to find, remove or move the libraries you have.

Why a Photos library gets so big

The library is a special kind of folder that Photos manages for you. A few things make it grow faster than people expect:

  • Originals, kept in full. Unless you turn on Optimize Mac Storage, every photo and video is stored at full size on the Mac, alongside the smaller previews Photos makes so it can scroll quickly.
  • Video. A few minutes of 4K from a recent iPhone can take more room than a year of photos. Video is usually most of a big library.
  • Live Photos. Each one is a still plus a short video clip, so it takes more room than a plain photo.
  • Duplicates. The same pictures imported twice, from a camera card and then again from a phone, or from a folder that was imported more than once.
  • Imports that leave the originals behind. When Photos is set to copy items into the library (Photos → Settings → General), importing a folder of pictures from Downloads or an external drive makes a second copy. The folder you imported from is still there.
  • Recently Deleted. Deleting in Photos does not free space straight away. Apple says deleted items stay in the Recently Deleted album for 30 days before they are removed for good.
  • Old libraries. A library from a previous Mac, a copy made before a migration, an old iPhoto or Aperture library, or a second library someone made for a project. Photos only shows you the one that is open, so the others are easy to forget.

Libraries are not always in Pictures, either. Copies end up in Documents, on the Desktop, in Downloads after a restore, or on an external drive. What Optimize Mac Storage does, and when

If you use iCloud Photos, Optimize Mac Storage is the single biggest saving. Turn it on in Photos → Settings → iCloud. In Apple’s words, it “stores smaller versions of your photos on your Mac when storage space is limited”, while the full-size originals stay in iCloud. Opening a photo downloads the original again when you need it.

The catch is in the words when storage space is limited. Turning it on does not shrink the library straight away. While your disk has room, Photos may keep many originals on the Mac; it hands space back as the disk fills. So a library can look just as big the day after you switch it on. That is normal, not a fault.

Two other things to know. You need an iCloud plan big enough for your whole library, and an internet connection to open photos whose originals are only in iCloud. And if you want a full copy on the Mac again, the same settings page has Download Originals to this Mac, which needs the space back.

Clear space inside Photos, never in Finder

A Photos library looks like a file but holds thousands of files plus a database that keeps track of them. Moving or deleting anything from inside it by hand, in Finder or any other app, can damage the library. Always do this part in Photos:

  • Empty Recently Deleted. It is in the sidebar under Utilities. Check what is there first: once it is gone, it is gone.
  • Merge duplicates. Recent versions of Photos have a Duplicates album under Utilities that finds the same picture stored more than once and offers to merge them.
  • Look at your videos. The Videos view under Media Types gathers them in one place. Long clips you never watch again are usually the biggest wins.
  • Clean up the folders you imported from. If an import copied a folder into the library, the original folder is now a second copy. Klarity shows those folders in Downloads, Desktop or on an external drive; once you have checked the photos are in Photos, that copy can go.

Klarity backs up this rule. If you try to move something inside a Photos library to the Trash from Klarity, it shows a warning first and suggests deleting from inside the app instead.

Old and second libraries: check before you remove

An old library is often the easiest big win, but check three things first.

Make sure it is not the one you use. Quit Photos, then hold the Option key while you open it. Photos lists the libraries it knows about and lets you open the old one. In Photos → Settings → General you can see whether a library is the System Photo Library, the one that syncs with iCloud Photos. Apple warns that switching the System Photo Library turns off iCloud Photos, so switch back to your everyday library when you are done looking.

Make sure everything you want is in your current library. Scroll back through the old one. If it holds photos that never made it across, import them into your current library before you remove anything.

Expect less space than it shows if it was an iPhoto library. When Photos turns an old iPhoto library into a Photos library, the two can share the same photo files on the disk instead of keeping two copies. Klarity, like Finder, counts the files in each library, so removing the old iPhoto library may give back much less than its size suggests. A Photos library that was copied by hand is a real second copy, and removing it frees its full size.

Then remove it as a whole. Right-click the library in Klarity and choose Move to Trash. A library is almost always 1 GB or more, so Klarity asks for a deliberate slide to confirm rather than a click. It only ever moves things to the Trash, so nothing is deleted until you empty the Trash yourself; give it a few days if you want to be sure.

Move the library to an external drive, the supported way

If you do not use iCloud Photos, or your library is simply bigger than your Mac, an external drive is the honest answer. Apple’s steps are short:

  1. Quit Photos.
  2. In Finder, open Pictures (or wherever your library is) and drag Photos Library to the external drive.
  3. When the copy finishes, double-click the library in its new place to open it.
  4. If you use iCloud Photos, make it the System Photo Library in Photos → Settings → General.
  5. Once you have checked it opens and everything is there, you can move the original in Pictures to the Trash and empty it.

Apple’s conditions matter. The drive must be formatted as APFS or Mac OS Extended (Journaled), it cannot be the drive you use for Time Machine backups, and Apple advises against SD cards, USB flash drives, network drives and cloud folders. Keep the drive connected before you open Photos; if Photos cannot find it, it makes a new, empty library in Pictures, which can look alarming but loses nothing. Keep a backup of the external drive too: one copy on one drive is not a backup. Once the library lives there, what to do when the external drive fills up covers the next stage.

On a small Mac this is often the move that matters most. Living with a 256 GB Mac covers the monthly habit that keeps the rest of the disk in check.

Questions people ask

Why is my Photos library so big on my Mac?

Usually because full-size originals are stored on the Mac, video takes far more room than photos, and Live Photos add a short clip to each picture. Duplicates, items waiting 30 days in Recently Deleted, and older libraries left in Pictures or elsewhere add to it.

Does Optimize Mac Storage shrink the Photos library right away?

Not necessarily. Apple says it stores smaller versions of your photos on the Mac when storage space is limited, with the full-size originals in iCloud. While the disk has room, Photos may keep many originals on the Mac, so the library can stay large for a while after you turn it on.

Is it safe to delete an old Photos or iPhoto library?

Yes, if it is not your System Photo Library and everything you want is in your current library. Open it first by holding Option while you open Photos. Remove the library as a whole, never pieces from inside it. An old iPhoto library may share files with your Photos library, so removing it can free less space than its size suggests.

Can Klarity find duplicate photos?

No. Klarity does not look inside your Photos library for duplicates. It shows how big each library is and how long since it changed, so you can spot forgotten libraries. To merge duplicate photos, use the Duplicates album under Utilities in Photos.
