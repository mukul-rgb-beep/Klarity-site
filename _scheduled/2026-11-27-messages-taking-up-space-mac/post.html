Messages Taking Up Space on Your Mac? Clear It Safely

Years of photos, videos and voice messages can turn Messages into one of the biggest things on your Mac. How to see how big it really is, and how to clear it without breaking your conversations.

Why Messages grows so big

Open System Settings → General → Storage and you may find Messages near the top of the list, sometimes at 20 or 40 GB. That surprises people, because a text message is tiny. The words are not the problem. Everything sent along with them is.

Every photo in the family group, every video of a birthday, every voice message, sticker, GIF, PDF and screenshot your friends sent you is kept as a file on your Mac. Unless you have changed it, Messages keeps all of it forever. Over five or ten years of group chats, that adds up quietly, and none of it shows up in Finder, so you never see it pile up.

Those files live in a folder called Messages inside your Library folder, which macOS hides. Most of the bulk is in a folder inside it called Attachments. You are not meant to manage it by hand, and as you will see below, you should not. But knowing it is there explains where the space went.

What Storage settings tells you, and what it does not

Storage settings gives you one number for Messages. That is useful: it tells you whether Messages is worth your time at all. If it says 800 MB, close the window and look elsewhere. If it says 30 GB on a 256 GB Mac, it is one of the biggest things on your disk.

What one number cannot tell you is how that compares with everything else, or how old it is. Is Messages your biggest problem, or is it the third-biggest after a forgotten video project and an old phone backup? Is it 30 GB of last month’s holiday photos that you still want, or 30 GB of chats from 2019? Those lead to very different decisions.

Clear it the right way: inside Messages

Once you know Messages is worth tackling, do the clearing in Messages itself, or in Storage settings, which hands the job to Messages. Both keep your conversation history in step with the files. Save any photo or video you want to keep first: drag it out of the conversation to a folder, or into Photos.

1. Remove the biggest attachments first

On recent versions of macOS, open System Settings → General → Storage and click the ⓘ button next to Messages. You get a list of the attachments Messages is holding, which you can sort by size. Select the ones you do not need and click Delete. This is the fastest win: a handful of long videos often outweighs thousands of photos.

2. Tidy a single conversation

Inside a conversation, you can select a message or attachment, press Delete, and confirm. Apple’s guide notes that you can delete messages and attachments on an ongoing basis, so a busy group chat does not have to grow forever. To remove a whole conversation and everything in it, delete the conversation from the sidebar.

3. Tell Messages to stop keeping everything

Choose Messages → Settings, click General, and open the Keep messages menu. It is set to Forever unless you have changed it; the other choices are 1 Year and 30 Days. In Apple’s words, “If you choose an option other than Forever, your conversations (including all attachments) are automatically removed.”

That is the setting that stops the problem coming back, and also the one to be most careful with. It removes whole conversations, not just big files, so save anything you care about before you switch it. One year is a sensible middle ground for most people.

4. Remember Recently Deleted

Deleted messages are not gone straight away. Apple says you can view and recover items in the Recently Deleted folder for up to 30 days. That is a good safety net, and it also means the space may take a while to come back in full.

If you use Messages in iCloud

Many people have Messages in iCloud turned on, so the same conversations appear on their iPhone, iPad and Mac. That changes two things.

  • Deleting is everywhere. Apple is clear: “If you use Messages in iCloud, deleting a message or conversation on your Mac deletes it from all your devices.” Clearing out the Mac clears out your iPhone too. Usually that is what you want, but save first.
  • It is not an off switch for the Mac’s copy. Apple says keeping messages in iCloud can help save space on your Mac, but it does not give you a setting to keep attachments only in iCloud. If Messages is large on your Mac, deleting what you do not need is still the reliable way to shrink it.

You can check whether it is on in Messages → Settings → iMessage, where Enable Messages in iCloud is listed.

Why not just empty the Attachments folder?

It is tempting: find the folder, see that it is 30 GB, and move it to the Trash. Please don’t. Messages keeps its own record of every conversation, and that record points at each file in that folder. Take the files away behind its back and you get conversations full of blank spaces and broken images, while Messages still believes it has them. If Messages in iCloud is on, what happens next is out of your hands. Removing attachments through Messages, or through Storage settings, keeps the two in step.

Klarity is built with that in mind. Its job here is to show you how big Messages is and whether it is worth acting on, not to do the deleting. Klarity only ever moves things to the Trash and never empties it for you, and anything inside your Library folder gets an extra warning, “Apps keep their data and settings in your Library”, before the usual confirmation, with Cancel as the default. A folder of 1 GB or more also needs a deliberate slide to confirm. It is very hard to do this by accident, but the right answer for Messages is still to cancel and use Messages’ own tools.

WhatsApp keeps its own pile

If you use the WhatsApp app for Mac, its photos, videos and voice notes are not in Messages’ folder. WhatsApp keeps them in your Library folder too, under Group Containers, in folders whose names start with group.net.whatsapp. The same rule applies: clear media from inside WhatsApp, not by deleting files in that folder. As with Messages, macOS shields other apps’ data, so Klarity may show those folders as smaller than they really are. If WhatsApp is large on your phone, it is worth a look on the Mac too.

A quick routine

  1. Check the Messages figure in Storage settings. Under a gigabyte or two, move on.
  2. If it is large, scan your Library folder in Klarity to see how it compares with everything else, and how old it is.
  3. Save the photos and videos you want to keep.
  4. Delete the biggest attachments from Storage settings, then set Keep messages to 1 Year if you are comfortable with that.
  5. Leave the Attachments folder alone in Finder.

Questions people ask

Why is Messages taking up so much space on my Mac?

Because Messages keeps every photo, video, voice message and file anyone sends you, and by default it keeps them forever. Years of group chats can add up to tens of gigabytes. The files are stored in the Messages folder inside your Library folder, which macOS hides.

How do I delete Messages attachments on a Mac?

Open System Settings, then General, then Storage, and click the info button next to Messages to see the attachments it holds, sorted by size if you like. Select the ones you do not need and delete them. You can also delete attachments inside a conversation in Messages.

Can I delete the Messages Attachments folder in Finder?

It is better not to. Messages keeps a record of every conversation that points at those files, so deleting them by hand leaves broken images behind. Use Storage settings or Messages itself, which keep the two in step.

If I delete messages on my Mac, are they deleted on my iPhone?

Yes, if you use Messages in iCloud. Apple says deleting a message or conversation on your Mac then deletes it from all your devices. Save any photos or videos you want to keep before you start.
