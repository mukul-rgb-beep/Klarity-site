macOS 27.3 Beta: What Changes for Storage

Apple is testing the next update to macOS 27. Here is what its release notes say about storage so far, the one change that touches your files, and why the beta is no way to get space back.

The short answer

So far, nothing in Apple’s notes for the 27.3 beta is about your disk space. Apple’s release notes for the second beta have no item on disk space, System Data, Time Machine, snapshots or iCloud Drive. The change that does touch your files is about permissions: how much of your disk an app may read when you give it Full Disk Access.

macOS 27.3 Golden Gate is still in testing, and Apple has not said when it will be released. Everything below about 27.3 comes from Apple’s notes for the second beta. Beta notes describe a work in progress, so any of it can change before the release. I will update this post when 27.3 ships.

Full Disk Access gets narrower

Some apps, including some disk tools and cleaners, ask for Full Disk Access so they can read everything on your Mac at once. In 27.3, Apple’s notes list among the changes:

“Guidance to encourage users to grant access to just the specific apps (for System & App Protection Override) or folders (for Full Disk Access) that are required for their workflows”

In plain words: when an app asks, macOS will nudge you to give it only the folders it needs, not the whole disk. Two more lines from the same notes matter if you already use such an app:

  • “Users will see these updates when approving new app permission requests.”
  • “All app permissions previously granted by users will remain in effect.”

So, as the beta stands, nothing you have already allowed is taken away. Apple also tells developers to make sure their apps can cope with access to “only a limited set of folders”, and it says apps already known to it are not affected for now. Other apps that expect the whole disk may work differently for people who install them after 27.3.

A disk map that never needed Full Disk Access

Klarity, the Mac disk space analyzer I build, already works the way Apple is now encouraging. It never asks for Full Disk Access. It runs in Apple’s sandbox, and you give it access to a folder or a disk once, the first time you scan it. Then it draws that folder or disk as a map: every folder is a rectangle, its size on screen is the space it takes, and its colour says how long since it last changed.

One honest limit: Klarity cannot see inside APFS snapshots or Time Machine backups. It shows the files and folders you give it access to, and that is the space you can clear yourself. It only ever moves things to the Trash, and asks first every time.

One smaller change: more Rosetta notices

The beta notes say “macOS now notifies users when an audio plug-in or print dialog extension that requires Rosetta is used”, and it does the same when you print with a printer driver that needs Rosetta. If you see one, this guide to Intel apps on macOS 27 explains what it means.

Should you install the beta to get space back?

No. Nothing in the notes frees space, and a beta is the wrong place to look for it. Apple’s own advice in its beta programme FAQ is to “Install the beta software only on non-production devices that are not business critical”, and to back up with Time Machine first.

If your Mac is on the beta and you want to leave, Apple’s steps are: open System Settings → General → Software Update, click the Info button next to Beta updates, and choose Off. Your Mac then gets no more betas. When the next regular version of macOS is released, you can install it from Software Update.

Questions people ask

Does macOS 27.3 free up storage?

Not according to Apple so far. The release notes for the second beta have no item about disk space, System Data, Time Machine, snapshots or iCloud Drive. That can change before the release.

Will macOS 27.3 take away Full Disk Access from apps I already allowed?

Not as the beta stands. Apple’s notes say “All app permissions previously granted by users will remain in effect.” The new guidance appears when an app asks for permission after the update. That can change before the release.

When will macOS 27.3 be released?

Apple has not said. It is in beta now, and this post will be updated when it ships.

Does Klarity need Full Disk Access?

No. It runs in Apple’s sandbox and asks for access to a folder or a disk once, the first time you scan it.
