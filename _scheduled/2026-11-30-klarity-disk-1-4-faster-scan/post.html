Coming in Klarity Disk 1.4: a scan about 4× faster

In our tests, the scanner planned for version 1.4 runs the default scan of a folder of half a million files in 0.66 seconds instead of 2.74, and a full-depth scan uses under half the memory. Here are the numbers, and what they do not show yet.

The short version

The next version of Klarity Disk, 1.4, scans much faster. I have rebuilt the part of Klarity that measures your disk. In our tests, the default scan of a folder with half a million files (the scan Klarity runs when you pick a folder) took 0.66 seconds instead of 2.74, about four times faster. A full-depth scan, which goes many levels deeper (Scan Depth set to Full in Settings), took 0.80 seconds instead of 4.10, about five times faster, and it used under half the memory.

One thing first: 1.4 is not out yet. These are results from the new scanner in our tests, not from an app you can download today, and I will confirm the final figures when 1.4 is released.

Why the scan is worth speeding up

Klarity Disk is the Mac disk space analyzer I build. It draws a folder or a whole disk as a map: every folder is a rectangle as big as the space it takes, coloured by how long since anything in it changed. Before it can draw anything, it has to look at every file and folder and add them up. That is the scan, and it is the one part of the app where you sit and wait.

You also rarely scan once. You scan your home folder, open the biggest folder to see inside it, move something to the Trash, and Klarity scans again to show what you got back. Each of those is a wait, so a shorter one adds up over a clean-up.

What we tested

One test folder, built to look like a home folder: 518,036 files in 41,104 folders. It ran on one Mac: a MacBook Pro with an M5 Max chip and a 2 TB SSD, running macOS 27.3. Every figure is the middle result of five runs. The default, full-depth and disk-image scans ran under the same App Sandbox rules as Klarity Disk from the Mac App Store, so in those three tests the new scanner had no access the real app does not have.

“Opening a big folder” is what happens when you double-click a folder in the map that the first scan did not go inside: Klarity scans it on the spot. The folder in that test held 217,878 files.

Same answer, just sooner

Faster is no use if the answer changes, so I checked that too. On the test folder, both scanners found the same number of files, 518,036, and the same total size, 847,962,112 bytes, to the byte. The totals you see should be the same, after a shorter wait.

Less memory too

In our tests, at its busiest, the full-depth scan used about 260 MB instead of 647 MB, under half. That leaves more room for the apps you are using while it runs, which matters most on a Mac with little memory to spare.

Where you should notice it most

  • Scanning your home folder. This is the default scan, the one that was about four times faster in our tests.
  • Opening big folders. A big folder deeper down, such as one inside Library or Downloads, that the first scan did not go inside. Opening a big folder was about three times faster.
  • Full-depth scans. If you set Scan Depth to Full to see deeper at once, that scan gained the most: about five times.
  • Scanning again after a clean-up. After you move something to the Trash, or press ⌘R, Klarity scans again. That is the same kind of scan, so it should be quicker too, though we did not time it separately.
  • External drives, less so. Slower storage gains less: about 2.7 times on the disk image. Finding what fills an external drive still works the same way, likely with a shorter wait.

The honest limits

  • One folder, one Mac. Your own gain depends on your Mac, your disk and how many files you have. “About four times” is what we measured on the test folder, not a promise for every Mac. A MacBook Pro with an M5 Max is a fast Mac, so on an older or smaller Mac the times may be longer, with both the old and the new scanner.
  • Slower storage gains less. The same files on a disk image, standing in for slower storage, went from 5.46 to 2.03 seconds: about 2.7 times faster, not four. Expect external and slower drives to gain less than the SSD figures above.
  • Spinning hard drives have not been measured yet. I will not guess a figure for them.
  • 1.4 has no release date yet. When it is out, I will confirm the figures from the released app.
